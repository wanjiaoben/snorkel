A practical guide for first-time visitors: where to meet, who should not join, how to prepare for seasickness, what to bring, and how weather decisions are made.

Meeting at Ginowan Marina

For the shared Kerama-area snorkeling trip, we meet at Ginowan Marina. Parking at the marina is ¥600 per use. The usual morning schedule is 8:00 AM meeting, 8:15 departure, and return around 12:30.

The boat ride to the islands takes about 30–40 minutes. You will spend around two hours in the water, with regular breaks on board. Exact timing is confirmed the evening before, around 6 PM, depending on sea conditions.

Health and fitness requirements

Snorkeling looks easy from shore, but it still requires comfort in open water, enough energy to climb back onto the boat, and the ability to follow staff instructions calmly.

Ages 2–69 may enter the water. Guests aged 70 and above are welcome to join the tour and enjoy the trip from the boat.

Guests who are pregnant, or who have high blood pressure or diabetes requiring daily medication, cannot join. Everyone completes a standard medical questionnaire and signs a confirmation form on the day; guardians sign for minors.

Non-swimmers are welcome if they are comfortable in the water. Life jackets are worn at all times and staff stay close. People who panic when their feet leave the bottom should not join.

Seasickness and the 4-hour shared boat timeline

If you get seasick easily, take motion sickness medication before departure, not after you already feel unwell. Bring water and light snacks, and avoid alcohol the night before.

Gear, clothes and what to bring

Basic snorkeling gear is included: mask, snorkel, fins and a life jacket worn at all times. Prescription mask lenses are available in approximate strengths. For summer, a long-sleeve rash guard is usually enough and helps protect against sun and scrapes.

Bring

  • Swimwear worn under your clothes
  • Towel and sandals that are easy to slip on and off
  • Drinks, light snacks and seasickness medication
  • Reef-friendly waterproof sunscreen; do not use sunscreen mud

After the tour

Hot showers are available at the marina for ¥300. Keep luggage minimal because the boat and marina are not places to manage large bags.

Marine life and reef manners

Do not touch coral, fish, turtles or any other marine life. Do not stand on reef. Give animals space and follow staff instructions if jellyfish, sea urchins or other hazards are nearby.

If something stings or scratches you, tell staff immediately instead of waiting until the end of the tour.

Weather decisions, cancellation and refunds

The captain decides whether the boat sails. If sea or weather conditions make the tour impossible, such as typhoon conditions, you can reschedule or receive a full refund.

Guest cancellation fees are: 14+ days before, full refund; 13–10 days, 50% fee; 9–6 days, 80% fee; within 5 days, no refund. Personal illness such as a cold or upset stomach is not covered by the weather policy. Visa refusal receives a full refund.

Cameras, GoPro and valuables

You may bring a GoPro or camera, but secure it with a strap or float. The boat is wet and moving, and small items can disappear quickly. Valuables are your own responsibility, so bring only what you need for the trip.
